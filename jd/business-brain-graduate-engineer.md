## About MonoEdge

MonoEdge builds an advanced intelligence layer for the Indian mid-market
manufacturing sector. Our software behaves like an always-on analyst rather than
another dashboard: it reads what is happening across a plant, connects
production data to quality, energy, and cost, and tells the people running the
floor what is going wrong and why — in English, Hindi, and Marathi.

We are a technically-driven, bootstrapped company defining a new category in
industrial optimization. The person we hire will help take our core product, the
*Operations Intelligence (Business Brain)*, live on real factory floors.

## About the role

The Operations Intelligence (Business Brain) is the decision layer of our product. None of it matters
until it is running on a real line — and this role is how it gets there.

As a Graduate Engineer, you will travel to customer plants, stand our system up
alongside their machines and automation partners, connect it to their PLCs, and
then stay close to the data it produces to make sure the answers it gives are
true. It is a hands-on, travelling role for someone who is as comfortable
reading a PLC tag list as they are opening a dataset and asking why a number
moved.

This is a rare opportunity for a fresher to see the whole arc — from a raw
signal on a factory floor to a decision a plant owner acts on — while learning
industrial automation, real data analytics, and applied machine learning from a
senior team that has shipped all three.

## What you will do

- **Deploy on-site.** Travel to customer plants, set up our hardware and
  software next to live production, and get the Operations Intelligence (Business Brain) reading real data
  within days.
- **Work with PLCs and automation partners.** Coordinate with the customer's
  controls team and their automation vendors to read the right tags over OPC UA
  / Modbus, agree on what each signal means, and integrate cleanly without
  disrupting the line.
- **Run root cause analysis.** When rejects spike or a batch goes bad, dig into
  the data with the plant team to find the actual cause, not the first plausible
  story.
- **Do data analytics.** Pull, clean, and join messy industrial data; build the
  charts and summaries that make a problem legible; and check that what the
  system reports matches what the floor sees.
- **Support the machine learning work.** Help the senior team prepare labelled
  data, sanity-check model predictions against ground truth, and flag when a
  model has drifted in production.
- **Write things down.** Document what you learn at each site so the next
  deployment is faster than the last.

## Who should apply

We are looking for a recent graduate in **any engineering discipline**. What
matters is the mix of the physical and the analytical, not the exact branch.

You are a strong fit if you have:

- A genuine willingness to travel and to work on a plant floor — hot, loud, and
  unglamorous. Spending a week commissioning a system at a factory should sound
  good, not off-putting.
- Some basic coding knowledge: you can write a simple **Python** script and are
  comfortable working with data. Production-grade software experience is not
  required — the willingness to learn matters more.
- A basic, honest grasp of **machine learning**: training vs. testing,
  overfitting, and why a model that scores well on paper can still be wrong on
  the floor. Depth is not expected — curiosity and clear thinking are.
- A root-cause temperament: when something breaks, you want to know why, and you
  keep asking until the explanation actually fits the evidence.
- Clear communication in English, and ideally Hindi or Marathi, so you can talk
  to a shift supervisor and a founder in the same day.

**Nice to have** (none of these are required): any exposure to PLCs, SCADA, OPC
UA, or Modbus; a final-year or personal project in data analytics or machine
learning; time spent in a factory, workshop, or industrial internship;
familiarity with Git and the command line.

> **A note on marks:** we do not screen on CGPA alone. A strong project you can
> explain, and clear thinking in the interview, count for more than a decimal
> point.
